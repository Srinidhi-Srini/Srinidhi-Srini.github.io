Hello everyone. This post will be on the Kirchhoff's Current and Loop Laws, equations used to solve various circuit values. First, let's briefly talk about what a circuit is.

## Circuits
A circuit is a closed and unbroken loop of conductive material that allows current to pass through. It's powered by something like a voltage or current source. Voltage (V) is what pushes current throughout the circuit. It's the potential energy difference between two points. Current (I) is the flow of electric charge. Resistance (R) slows down or blocks current from flowing. We can quantify the relationship between these components using Ohm's Law: V=IR.

{: style="text-align: center;"}

But, most of the time, circuits aren't as simple as the one above. There may be multiple resistors, paths, currents and voltages we need to find. To make circuits easier to solve, the primary step is combining all the resistors into one resistor source.

### Resistors in Series
Resistors in series are those that are connected end-to-end, and the same current flows through them. 

![R_series circuit](/assets/images/bpp5.png){: style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;"}
{: style="text-align: center;"}

Here, let's say $$ R_{1} = 5 \Omega$$, $$ R_{2} = 10 \Omega$$ and $$ R_3 = 15 \Omega$$. These 3 resistors are in series, so $$R_{eq} = R_{1} + R_{2} + R_{3}$$, which is 5 + 10 + 15 = 25 $$\Omega$$. We can then rewrite this as a singular resistor with resistance $$ R_{eq} $$. 

### Resistors in Parallel
Resitors in parallel are those connected across the same 2 nodes. A node is a point where two or more circuit elements meet. They have the same voltage, but different current. 

![R_parallel circuit](/assets/images/bpp6.png){: style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;"}
{: style="text-align: center;"}

Again, let's say $$ R_{1} = 5 \Omega$$, $$ R_{2} = 10 \Omega$$ and $$ R_3 = 15 \Omega$$. These 3 resistors are in series, so $$R_{eq} = \frac{1}{R_{1}} + \frac{1}{R_{2}} + \frac{1}{R_{3}}$$, which is 5 + 10 + 15 = 25 $$\Omega$$. We can then rewrite this as a singular resistor with resistance $$ R_{eq} $$.
