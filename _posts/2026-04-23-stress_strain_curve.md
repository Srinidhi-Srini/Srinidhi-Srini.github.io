Hello everyone. This post will be about the Stress-Strain Curve, a very important figure in Materials Engineering. What is a Stress-Strain curve? As the name may suggest, it's a graph that shows the behavior of a material under a load, with stress being the y-axis and strain being the x-axis. From the graph, we can determine more properties of the material, like stiffness, strength, ductility, etc. We will cover all of these concepts in this blog post.

## Stress & Strain
First, what is stress and strain? Stress is defined as the internal force per unit area ($$\sigma = \frac{F}{A_0} $$). It's important to note that the Area used is the original cross sectional Area before any load is applied. A force in general is any external push or pull on an object. One example of stress is columns supporting a building, with compressive stress in the columns resulting from the forces exerted on it by the building. 

Strain is a measurement of deformation, defined as the ratio of the change in length to the original length ($$ \varepsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L_0}$$). One example of strain is the previously mentioned column shortening due to being under load of the building. 


## Elastic Deformation

Elastic Deformation is defined as reversible and nonpermanent deformation. If you apply a load, the material will deform, but after removing it, it will go back to how it was before. The bonds of the atoms stretch when the load is applied, and after removing it, they unstretech. This is a linear relationship between stress and strain. The slope of this line (E) is Young's Modulus. Young's Modulus E measures a material's resistance to elastic deformation, or Stiffness.

## Plastic Deformation & Yield Strength

Plastic Deformation is defined as nonreversible and permanent deformation. If you apply a load, the material will deform, and after removing it, it will still be deformed. The bonds of the atom will stretch and planes of atoms will shift when the load is applied, and after removing it, the bonds unstretch but the planes of atoms remain shifted. The Yield Strength ($$ \sigma_y $$) is the point of noticable plastic deformation. On the image below, it's the orange portion. Plastic Deformation is the green portion.

## Tensile Strength

Tensile Strength is the maximum stress the material can undergo before fracturing. It's also the point where necking begins. Necking is this type of deformation where it essentially deforms inwards towards the center. Eventually, the necking results in fracture and that is the end of the curve. Ductility measures how much plastic deformation a material can undergo before fracturing.
